After-tax module

Verified federal and California rules reduced to one comparable number — with every omitted rule stated out loud.

The after-tax module turns verified rules into one number

The purpose of this page is narrow: take the rules that were read from official publications and reduce them to a single after-tax figure the owner can hold next to the hold case. It does not need the property to explain the rules, and it is explicit about which rules it refuses to apply because they could not be verified.

What this module deliberately does not do

Excluded rules — and the direction of the error

  • California alternative minimum tax — the threshold is published ($92,749 single or head of household, $123,667 joint, $61,830 separate) but the computation depends on Schedule P (540) preference items from the owner's full return, so it is not modelled. TX-16
  • Property-tax reassessment on sale — a cost to the buyer and therefore to the achievable price. Not modelled. TX-11
  • FTB Form 593 withholding at closing — not verified, not modelled. TX-12
  • Section 199A and Opportunity Zone provisions, and itemised deductions, credits and exemption credits — not verified in this session, so not applied.
  • Any depreciation recapture beyond the 25% unrecaptured ceiling — not verified, so not applied.

Every one of these omissions is listed in data/rules.json under excluded, so the gap travels with the code rather than living in someone's memory.

Suggested improvements

  • Verify and add the mental-health surcharge — the single largest known omission in the after-tax number.
  • Add a side-by-side installment-sale scenario, since spreading gain across years can move it into lower brackets and IRS Publication 537 confirms the method is available.
  • Add an exchange-versus-sale comparison once a replacement property is identified, remembering that an exchange defers rather than eliminates the tax.
  • Have a CPA review the encoded brackets against the filed return before the number is used in a commitment.
