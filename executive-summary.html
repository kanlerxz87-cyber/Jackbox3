Findings

Executive summary

What the verified record supports, what it does not, and the action sequence that follows from the gaps.

The one-sentence answer

There is no defensible sell-or-renew answer yet, and the reason is specific rather than vague: the sale case cannot be computed without a market value and a basis, and the renewal case cannot be computed without the lease terms. What can be said, and is said below with sources, is that the surrounding economy is flat rather than collapsing, the tenant's store-level sales are declining and its unit count is shrinking, and the reference location carries a very high FEMA composite risk rating driven by seismic exposure. Those facts argue for a particular sequence of work, not for a particular outcome.

What the verified record actually shows

1. The local economy is flat — not booming, not breaking

Los Angeles MSA nonfarm employment averaged 6,281.3 thousand in 2025 against 6,288.0 thousand in 2024: a change of −0.1%. That is stagnation, not decline. The MSA unemployment rate was 4.4% in September 2026 against 5.3% for the 2025 annual average and a 6.1% cycle peak in mid-2024, so the labour market has been improving even while payrolls went sideways. Nationally the rate was 4.1% in October 2026 and California stood at 5.0%. The Federal Reserve's October 2025 Beige Book described activity as having "changed little on balance", with labour demand "generally muted".

What this supports: no evidence of an imminent demand collapse at metro level. What it does not support: any claim of growth that would justify an aggressive escalation assumption in a renewal. Flat payrolls plus rising food-away-from-home prices (+3.70% in 2025 and +3.46% year over year to September 2026) describe a market where the tenant is recovering price while losing transactions — which is exactly what the tenant reports.

2. Neighborhood evidence is not decision-grade yet

The parcel address is intentionally absent from this public site. Prior tract and county ACS observations came from a third-party API mirror; they have been withdrawn because this audit could not independently confirm their table values from a direct official Census response. No neighborhood income, rent, poverty, education, population, or tenure conclusion is asserted here. A private adviser must select consistent Census geographies from the actual parcel location and verify the applicable ACS tables, vintages, and margins of error before using them.

What this supports: a requirement to complete the secure W-01 geography and official-data check before using neighborhood evidence. What it does not support: a demand, rent-growth, customer-income, or renewal conclusion based on the prior mirror values.

3. The tenant is the credit, and the credit is weakening

Jack in the Box Inc. reported system same-store sales of −1.1% for the quarter ended 5 July 2026, following −7.1% in the year-earlier quarter. Total revenues fell 1.8% to $257.7 million. The restaurant count went from 2,168 to 2,115 — 53 net closures, or −2.4% — with 4 openings and 17 closures in the quarter alone, attributed in part to the company's "JACK on Track" closure programme. For fiscal 2025 the company reported a net loss of $80.7 million and adjusted EBITDA of $270.9 million, down from $322.3 million. Total revenue fell 6.7% from fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2025, though that comparison is distorted by the December 2025 sale of Del Taco into discontinued operations.

What this supports: a renewal case must be underwritten on franchisee store-level economics and a guarantee, not on the parent's brand. A shrinking system means the tenant has less need for any individual site, which weakens the owner's position at expiry. What it does not support: any prediction that this store will close. Nothing public identifies it.

4. The reference location carries high hazard exposure, mostly seismic

FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer places the reference point in Zone X, "Area of Minimal Flood Hazard" — the lowest category. CAL FIRE's Local Responsibility Area layer returns no polygon there, though a positive control at Griffith Park correctly returns "Very High", which proves the query works rather than that the site is fire-free. The USGS ASCE 7-16 service puts short-period spectral acceleration SDS at 1.311 g with seismic design category D and a site-adjusted PGA of 0.843 g. FEMA's National Risk Index rates the tract "Very High" overall, with expected annual loss to buildings of $3.65 million across the tract, an expected annual loss rate of 0.218% of building value, and — importantly — community resilience "Very Low".

What this supports: earthquake, not wildfire or flood, is the dominant physical risk, and slow recovery capacity argues for a larger loss-of-rent reserve than a generic assumption would produce. What it does not support: any dollar figure, because the expected-loss rate needs a verified replacement cost and the repository has none.

5. The after-tax case can be computed, but not yet meaningfully

The rules are now pinned to official publications: federal long-term capital gains at 0/15/20% with 2026 thresholds of $49,450 and $545,500 for a single filer; unrecaptured section 1250 gain at a maximum 25%; net investment income tax at 3.8% above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint; the section 1250 recapture taxed at the lesser of the ordinary marginal rate and a 25% ceiling; California treating the gain as ordinary income up to 12.3%, with a 1% surcharge above $1,000,000 and a standard deduction of only $5,706 single; and the installment method available under IRS Publication 537. What is missing is everything property-specific: basis, depreciation history, mortgage payoff and sale price. The calculator will do the arithmetic the moment those exist.

The recommended course of action

This is a sequence, not a verdict. It is chosen so that no irreversible commitment is made before the information needed to judge it exists.

  1. Do not list, and do not sign a renewal, in the next 90 days. Nothing in the verified record is urgent enough to justify acting blind, and the lease has at least four years to run on the most conservative reading.
  2. Assemble the four private documents — lease, depreciation schedules and basis, mortgage statement, insurance declarations — into the access-controlled workspace. These are the only things that unlock the numbers.
  3. Commission the two professional opinions that cannot be self-served: an appraisal, and a structural and seismic report. Both are cheap relative to the decision.
  4. Then run both cases in the private calculator with real inputs, and compare after-tax net proceeds from a sale against the present value of the renewal case, including an empty-period allowance.
  5. Only then choose — and choose against a reserve price rather than a target price.

What would change this recommendation

  • A lease clause giving the tenant a below-market renewal option it will certainly exercise — that removes most of the owner's optionality and pushes toward selling now.
  • A very low basis with large accumulated depreciation — that makes a sale expensive and pushes toward holding, or toward an installment or exchange structure.
  • A mortgage maturing before 2030 — that makes the timing decision about the loan rather than the lease.
  • Any indication that this store is on the closure list — that would make the next 24 months the right selling window.
