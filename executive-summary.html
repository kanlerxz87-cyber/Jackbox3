Findings

Executive summary

What the verified record supports, what it does not, and the action sequence that follows from the gaps.

The one-sentence answer

There is no defensible sell-or-renew answer yet, and the reason is specific rather than vague: the sale case cannot be computed without a market value and a basis, and the renewal case cannot be computed without the lease terms. What can be said, and is said below with sources, is that the surrounding economy is flat rather than collapsing, the tenant's store-level sales are declining and its unit count is shrinking, and the reference location carries a very high FEMA composite risk rating driven by seismic exposure. Those facts argue for a particular sequence of work, not for a particular outcome.

What the verified record actually shows

1. The local economy is flat overall, with QSR labour-cost pressure

Los Angeles MSA nonfarm employment averaged 6,281.3 thousand in 2025 against 6,288.0 thousand in 2024 (−0.1%). CA EDD reports Los Angeles County seasonally adjusted unemployment improving to 4.6% in October 2026 (MSA unadjusted 4.4% in September 2026), with Limited-Service Restaurants (NAICS 722513) holding 193,200 jobs (+1.6% YoY) inside an $802.0B county economy (BEA 2023 real GDP, #1 in the U.S.). At the same time, California's statutory minimum wage rose to $16.90/hr on January 1, 2026 while AB 1228 keeps the fast-food floor at $20.00/hr — directly impacting store margins where 41.3% of Jack in the Box units sit in California.

What this supports: no evidence of an imminent metro demand collapse. What it does not support: aggressive rent-escalation assumptions. Flat payrolls plus +3.46% menu-price inflation and a $20/hr fast-food wage floor match what the tenant reports: higher menu prices offsetting fewer transactions.

2. The trade area is not the county, and the difference matters

Using Census Tract 2074 as the trade-area proxy for central Los Angeles: median household income is $78,482 (margin of error ±$29,379) against $90,112 county-wide (87.1%). Median gross rent is $3,154 against $1,954 county-wide (1.61×). Poverty is 29.4% against 13.7%, tenure is 100% renter-occupied, and income is bimodal (22.6% ≥$150k vs 27.1% <$25k). At macro level, recent net out-migration (−3.2% County 2020–2025 per QuickFacts V2025; −0.6% YoY per DOF Jan 2026 E-1) diverges from SCAG's long-range 2019–2050 adopted projection (+22.5% households, +7.4% population).

What this supports: a dense, all-rental catchment with bifurcated spending power. What it does not support: assuming uniform central-LA affluence or anchoring rent solely on tract income (37.4% MOE).

3. The tenant is the credit, and the Q3 FY26 10-Q adds hard numbers

Jack in the Box Inc. reported system same-store sales of −1.1% in Q3 FY2026 (−4.2% for 40 weeks YTD), following −7.1% in Q3 FY2025. Total unit count fell by 53 net units to 2,115 (149 company, 1,966 franchised). Crucially for a NNN landlord, the Q3 FY26 10-Q (Note 5 & Note 7) shows franchise variable percentage rent fell 18.0% YTD ($66.3m vs $80.8m), compressing net franchise rental spread by 15.4%, while standard leases run 20 years plus 1–20 year sole-discretion tenant renewal options. Total securitized debt stands at $1,492.0m (including $500.0m Series 2026-1 at 7.624%), with ARDs clustering in August 2029 ($426.4m), May 2031 ($500.0m), and February 2032 ($503.3m) plus a $23.3m mandatory cash sweep — lining up directly with the 2030–2035 lease window.

What this supports: underwriting must look through to unit-level sales, franchisee credit, and exact option terms (GATE-01/08). What it does not support: predicting closure of this unnamed unit.

4. Hazard exposure is seismic-driven, with quantified 30-yr rupture risk

FEMA's NFHL places the reference point in Zone X (minimal flood). CAL FIRE's LRA layer returns no polygon at Civic Center while a Griffith Park control correctly returns "Very High". USGS ASCE 7-16 puts SDS at 1.311 g (Design Category D; PGA 0.843 g), USGS UCERF3 places 30-year Los Angeles-area probabilities at 60% (M≥6.7), 46% (M≥7.0), and 31% (M≥7.5), and FEMA NRI rates the tract "Very High" composite risk with "Very Low" community resilience. California's FAIR Plan commercial backstop ($20M/building, $100M/location) currently sunsets January 1, 2028.

What this supports: earthquake loss-of-rent reserves and structural engineering verification (GATE-06/07/20). What it does not support: dollar loss estimates without verified building replacement cost (VA-08).

5. The after-tax & transfer-tax rules are ready; private basis is missing

Verified 2026 rules now include Rev. Proc. 2025-32 LTCG ceilings ($49,450 / $545,500 single; $98,900 / $613,700 joint), IRS Schedule D Tax Worksheet stacking of §1250 recapture (25% ceiling) beneath LTCG, 3.8% NIIT, California ordinary rates up to 12.3% plus the 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax above $1M, FTB Form 593 closing withholding (3⅓% gross or 12.3% gain election), Prop 13 reassessment (1.181% LA County FY24–25 average rate), and City of LA Measure ULA thresholds effective after June 30, 2026 (4.0% >$5.4M; 5.5% ≥$10.9M). The private calculator models outright sales, installment spreads, §1031 deferral, and W-02 Hold vs. Sell stress side by side.

The recommended course of action

This is a sequence, not a verdict. It is chosen so that no irreversible commitment is made before the information needed to judge it exists.

  1. Do not list, and do not sign a renewal, in the next 90 days. Nothing in the verified record is urgent enough to justify acting blind, and the lease has at least four years to run on the most conservative reading.
  2. Assemble the four private documents — lease, depreciation schedules and basis, mortgage statement, insurance declarations — into the access-controlled workspace. These are the only things that unlock the numbers.
  3. Commission the two professional opinions that cannot be self-served: an appraisal, and a structural and seismic report. Both are cheap relative to the decision.
  4. Then run both cases in the private calculator with real inputs, and compare after-tax net proceeds from a sale against the present value of the renewal case, including an empty-period allowance.
  5. Only then choose — and choose against a reserve price rather than a target price.

What would change this recommendation

  • A lease clause giving the tenant a below-market renewal option it will certainly exercise — that removes most of the owner's optionality and pushes toward selling now.
  • A very low basis with large accumulated depreciation — that makes a sale expensive and pushes toward holding, or toward an installment or exchange structure.
  • A mortgage maturing before 2030 — that makes the timing decision about the loan rather than the lease.
  • Any indication that this store is on the closure list — that would make the next 24 months the right selling window.
