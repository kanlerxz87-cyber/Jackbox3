Economy &amp; labour

A multi-year same-store view of payrolls, unemployment, prices and the tenant's own sales — direction rather than a single quarter.

Direction, not a single quarter

A single quarter tells you almost nothing about whether a trade area will support a restaurant through a lease term. What follows is a same-store-style series across several years for the things that drive a quick-service restaurant: payrolls, the unemployment rate, prices, and the tenant's own sales and unit count. Where a series is unavailable, it is absent rather than interpolated.

What the Federal Reserve says about the national backdrop

"Economic activity changed little on balance since the previous report… Employment levels were largely stable in recent weeks, and demand for labour was generally muted across Districts and sectors."

Federal Reserve Beige Book, National Summary, October 2025 (S26)

The same summary notes that lower- and middle-income households "continued to seek discounts and promotions in the face of rising prices", which is directly relevant to a value-oriented QSR in a tract where 27.1% of households earn under $25,000.

Building a projection system — and why none is published yet

The instruction was to build a reliable projection system for the area. The precondition attached to that instruction was "when verified inputs and sufficient comparable history exist". On this evidence they do not yet: the metro payroll series has no trend worth extrapolating, the tract-level ACS series cannot be pulled as a time series without an API key, and there is no store-level sales history for the site at all. A projection built on this would be a curve fitted to noise, and would be more dangerous than no curve because it would look like analysis.

What is published instead is the evidence a projection would have to explain, plus the specific series that would make one defensible:

  • Monthly MSA payroll employment and unemployment across the full post-2020 period, to establish a cycle rather than a point.
  • Store-level sales or traffic for the site, which only the franchisee holds.
  • Submarket rent history for comparable QSR space, which only a broker holds.
  • Population and household projections from the state Department of Finance or SCAG.

Suggested improvements

  • Obtain a Census API key so ACS can be pulled as a genuine multi-year tract-level series rather than read through a mirror of a single release.
  • Add BLS QCEW annual data for Los Angeles County by industry, which would show whether food-service employment itself is growing or shrinking.
  • Separate observations from assumptions in distinct columns when a projection is eventually published, so no reader can mistake one for the other.
