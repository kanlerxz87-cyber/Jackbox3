W-02 · Sell-versus-hold worksheet

Both cases costed with the same discipline, neither declared the winner, and an explicit list of the inputs that would break the tie.

Both cases, no winner

This page builds the sell case and the renew case with equal discipline and then stops. Selecting a winner now would require a market value, a basis and a rent — and all three are unverified. What the page can do is make the shape of each case explicit, so that when the missing inputs arrive the comparison is mechanical rather than argumentative.

The arithmetic each case needs

Sell case — the six numbers

  1. Gross sale price. From an appraisal and closed comparables. unverified
  2. Selling costs. Brokerage, transfer tax, legal. unverified
  3. Loan payoff including any prepayment penalty or defeasance. unverified
  4. Adjusted basis = original cost + capitalised improvements − depreciation taken or allowable. unverified
  5. Gain = amount realised − adjusted basis. Mechanical once items 1 and 4 exist.
  6. Tax = federal capital gain + section 1250 recapture + NIIT + California ordinary-rate tax on the gain. Encoded and ready.

Net after-tax proceeds = item 1 − 2 − 3 − 6. The calculator does items 5 and 6 today.

Renew case — the six numbers

  1. Renewal rent and escalation schedule, or market rent if the tenant will not renew. unverified
  2. Renewal term and the options the tenant holds. unverified
  3. Probability the tenant renews — with a shrinking system this is not 100%. unverified
  4. Empty period and tenant-improvement cost if it does not renew. unverified
  5. Holding costs: property tax, insurance, structural reserve. unverified
  6. Discount rate used for both cases so the comparison is like for like. unverified

Present value = discounted rent, net of holding costs and expected vacancy and improvement costs.

Why the discount rate matters more than the rent

A renewal case is a long stream of payments discounted to today. The choice of discount rate can move the present value by more than a realistic difference in rent, so it has to be agreed before the cases are run, and applied identically to both. If a sale is discounted at one rate and a hold at another, the comparison is theatre. That is GATE-19, and it is recorded here rather than buried.

Pricing the short-lease discount from real quotes

The request was to price the short-lease discount from real quotes rather than from a rule of thumb. Doing that properly requires three matched quotes: the rent a comparable QSR tenant pays on a new long lease in the same submarket; the rent the same space would fetch on a short remaining term; and the price per square foot at which a comparable building with a similar remaining term actually traded. None of the three is public. The honest output of this page is therefore the structure of the calculation, and an explicit statement that the quotes are missing — not a number with a plausible-looking provenance.

Suggested improvements

  • Build and back-test the cash-flow model only when the inputs exist. The instruction was to create a versioned cash-flow model when verified inputs and sufficient comparable history exist. They do not exist yet, so no model is published. Publishing an un-back-tested model would be worse than publishing none.
  • When it is built, version it and date every assumption, keeping observations in a separate column from assumptions so the two can never be confused.
  • Back-test it on at least one full market cycle of comparable submarket data before relying on it.
  • Set a reserve price, not a target price, and define in advance the number below which the owner walks.
