Education

Bachelor's degree or higher is 37.3% in the tract against 36.0% in the county — essentially at parity. The tract's 25+ population is 1,798 with a margin of error of ±352, so treat the comparison as approximate rather than exact.

Why the margin of error is not a footnote here

The tract median household income is $78,482 with a margin of error of ±$29,379. That is 37.4% of the estimate. It means the true value plausibly lies anywhere from roughly $49,000 to roughly $108,000 — a range that spans from well below to comfortably above the county median. Any conclusion that depends on the tract sitting at 87.1% of the county is therefore soft, and a decision should not rest on it. The county and metro figures have margins of a few hundred dollars and can carry weight.

The one comparison this page refuses to make

The ACS county "unemployment rate" derived here is 7.27%, and the current BLS rate is 4.4%. It would be easy to present those side by side as a trend. It would also be wrong: the ACS figure is a five-year average (2020–2024) that includes the pandemic, and the BLS figure is a current monthly estimate. They measure different things. Both are reported here, and the reader is told not to subtract one from the other.

Suggested improvements

  • Pull ACS one-year estimates for the tract where published, to get a current picture rather than a five-year blend.
  • Obtain a Census API key to build a genuine year-over-year tract series and settle population and income trend.
  • Add SCAG or state Department of Finance projections for the area, clearly labelled as projections rather than observations.
  • Widen the geography to a three-to-five tract catchment so a single ±236-household sample is not load-bearing.
