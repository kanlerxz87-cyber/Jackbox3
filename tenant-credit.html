Tenant & franchise credit

The tenant read from its own SEC filings: revenue, same-store sales, unit count and profitability, with the implications separated from the facts.

The tenant is the cash flow

Under a triple-net lease the owner receives rent and the tenant carries the operating risk. That makes the tenant's credit the owner's real asset. This page reads the tenant's own SEC filings rather than its marketing, and separates what the filings show from what they imply.

In the quarter itself, 4 restaurants opened and 17 closed. The company attributes part of the decline in franchise-level margin to "a decrease in the number of restaurants as part of the 'JACK on Track' closure program". A deliberate closure programme is materially different from random attrition: it means the tenant is actively pruning sites.

What the filings show, stated carefully

Negative for the owner

  • A net loss in FY2025 of $80.7 million, larger than the prior year's loss.
  • Adjusted EBITDA down 15.9% in a single year.
  • 53 net closures and an explicit closure programme.
  • Negative transactions, not merely negative sales.

Neutral or positive

  • The decline is decelerating sharply: −7.1% to −1.1% year over year.
  • Refinancing is described as complete, removing a near-term liquidity overhang.
  • The company is still opening restaurants, including franchised units.
  • Quarterly adjusted EBITDA improved to $61.2 million from $57.1 million year over year.

What this means for the renewal case

A tenant with a shrinking unit base has less need for any individual site, so the owner's negotiating leverage at expiry is weaker than a "national brand" story implies. That does not make renewal wrong. It changes how it should be structured: a renewal priced off the parent's brand rather than the store's economics is mispriced, and a franchisee-level guarantee becomes close to essential. The store-level sales figure that would settle this is held by the franchisee and is recorded as GATE-08.

What is deliberately not asserted

No credit rating, no outlook, no default probability, no debt-covenant analysis and no bond price. Rating-agency publications were not accessible, so TE-16 is blank. An 8-K was filed on 17 November 2026 under Item 5.02 (officer departure); it was identified but not read, so the company's current officer roster is recorded as TE-18 and marked flagged.

Suggested improvements

  • Read the FY2026 10-K when it is filed and rebuild this page from audited annual figures rather than quarterly releases.
  • Add the franchisor's disclosure document (the Franchise Disclosure Document), which contains unit-level financial performance representations in Item 19 where the company elects to provide them.
  • Add the store-level profit and loss from the franchisee — the single most informative private document for this decision.
  • Track the closure programme by market in each quarterly release, since a closure announced in Los Angeles would be the earliest public signal of risk to this specific site.
