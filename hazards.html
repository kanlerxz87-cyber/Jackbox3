W-04 · Parcel-level hazard screening

Regional hazard context replaced by the reference location's actual zone status for earthquake, fire and flood — with a positive control and an explicit list of what a point cannot answer.

What this page can and cannot tell you about the parcel

It screens the reference location, and it does so properly: every query is a live call to an official federal or state service, and one of them includes a positive control so you can see that the query works. But a point is not a parcel. Flood determinations, fire-zone designations and structural performance are all parcel-specific. This page gives you the screening result and then names the official document that replaces it.

The control test, shown in full

An empty result from a mapping query is ambiguous: it can mean "not in a zone" or "the query is broken". To tell the two apart, the same query was run against Griffith Park, which lies inside a mapped Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone. It returned LRA / incorporated / hazard code 3 / "Very High". The service works. The reference point genuinely has no mapped local very-high fire zone polygon — which is expected for a downtown block, and is still not a parcel-level fire clearance.

Reading the seismic numbers

SDS of 1.311 g and design category D describe a site with strong short-period shaking demand. Design category D is where a building must be detailed to remain stable under inelastic response. For an older, lightly reinforced or unreinforced-masonry retail box, that is exactly the condition under which retrofit obligations arise and insurance becomes expensive. The USGS response also returned null for the S1-based parameters at this site class while populating SDS. Those nulls are recorded as unverified. They were not back-filled from a neighbouring point, because a filled-in number would look exactly like a measured one.

Putting the hazards together: what actually threatens the decision

Business-as-usual risk

  • Earthquake is the dominant physical risk — not wildfire, not flood. Design category D plus a very high composite rating plus very low community resilience is a combination that says an event here takes the building offline for longer than a generic assumption allows for.
  • Loss of rent during repair is the real exposure for an owner whose only income from the asset is rent.
  • Insurance cost and availability are the transmission channel from hazard to valuation, and the premium is not in this repository.

Black-swan scenarios

  • A major regional earthquake that simultaneously damages the building, depresses the metro economy and freezes the transaction market — the scenario in which a planned sale cannot be executed at any price.
  • A regional wildfire and smoke season with utility shut-offs, which disrupts trade for weeks without touching the building.
  • An insurer withdrawal from the market, which can make the asset unfinanceable regardless of its physical condition.

All three share a property: they destroy liquidity and value at the same moment, which is why the reserve in GATE-20 must be sized before an event rather than after it.

Suggested improvements

  • Re-run all three services at the parcel centroid and at the parcel corners. Flood zones in particular can clip a rear lot or the access drive while missing the building footprint.
  • Add a repeatable script that takes coordinates and writes a dated hazard sheet, so the screen can be refreshed before every decision rather than being a one-off.
  • Add the USGS liquefaction and landslide layers once the service responds, plus the Alquist-Priolo fault-zone lookup for the parcel.
  • Record the CAL FIRE map version date alongside the result, since the zones are being remapped.
