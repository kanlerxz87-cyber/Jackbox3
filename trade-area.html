W-01 · Trade-area figures

City and county proxies replaced with Census-tract evidence, with margins of error shown on every estimate.

Why this page exists

An earlier framing of this analysis used city and county averages as a stand-in for the neighbourhood. That is the wrong unit of analysis for a restaurant: a county of ten million people is not a trade area, and a city of four million is not either. This page replaces those proxies with Census-tract figures and puts the margin of error next to every estimate, because at tract level a point estimate without its margin is close to meaningless.

What this changes about the decision

Findings that help the renewal case

  • Rents run far above the county. $3,154 against $1,954 is a 61% premium — real evidence of location value that a county average entirely hides.
  • A dense renter catchment. 100% renter-occupied tenure in the tract, with 2,598 residents packed into roughly a third of a square mile. Renters are the core quick-service customer.
  • High earners are present. 22.6% of households clear $150,000, and per capita income is 150% of the county, so there is a segment that will pay full menu price.

Findings that hurt the renewal case

  • Household income is below the county, not above it. 87.1% of the county median is the opposite of the intuition a "central Los Angeles" label creates.
  • Poverty is 29.4%, more than double the county. That constrains average ticket and makes the site dependent on value promotions — precisely the mix the tenant is retreating from.
  • The distribution is bimodal. A quarter rich and a quarter poor is a hard catchment to serve profitably with a single format.
  • The income margin of error is 37%. Even the below-county finding is soft.

What is still missing before this becomes a rent figure

Nothing on this page is a rent conclusion, and it should not be read as one. Converting a trade area into a rent requires three things this page does not have: a defined catchment boundary (GATE-14), traffic counts on the street the site fronts (GATE-13), and actual comparable rents paid by comparable tenants (GATE-05). The first two could in principle be assembled from public sources once the parcel address exists. The third cannot, because private commercial rents are not published by any official body.

Suggested improvements

  • Run the tract lookup at the parcel, not the Civic Center. One coordinate change re-runs every figure on this page; it is a five-minute task once the address exists.
  • Add ACS one-year estimates where the tract publishes them, so the 2020 pandemic years stop contaminating the five-year average.
  • Replace the single tract with a weighted ring of three to five tracts along the actual drive-time catchment, so one small sample does not drive the whole story.
  • Add competitor density from a licensed point-of-interest dataset and label it as non-official, or omit it. Do not substitute a web scrape for an official count.
